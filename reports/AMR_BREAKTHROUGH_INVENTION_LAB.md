## Part 1 — The missing questions

The three clusters were each required to first ask what the AMR industry *doesn't* ask. Their independent sets converged on a striking common core.

**From the physics cluster:**
1. What can a **moving** sensor measure that a fixed one structurally *cannot*? (→ spatial gradients, phase differences over a baseline, a synthetic aperture)
2. Can the robot's **own motion be the probe** — is movement the measurement mechanism, not just transport to a sensing site?
3. Can the robot **inject energy** (acoustic / EM / thermal / mechanical) and read the response — making the warehouse an interrogated sample rather than a passively-observed scene?
4. What does **repetition** reveal that a single pass cannot — drift, latent dynamics, differential baselines?
5. Can **physical contact** extract properties (stiffness, permittivity, thermal effusivity) no contactless sensor can reach?
6. Can **ambient fields already present** (RF, thermal, acoustic, magnetic) serve as free illumination, with the robot as the moving receiver?
7. Can **space be converted into time** — the robot's path as a delay line or giant aperture, to beat a sensor's intrinsic resolution?

**From the biology/science cluster:**
8. Can the robot **deliberately change the environment to learn something**, rather than merely observe it? (Intervention as measurement — `do()`-calculus made physical.)
9. Can **physical interaction** be an information-generating act, where an object's hidden state is read out through the body?
10. Can the fleet **discover relationships nobody specified**, rather than detect hazards someone predefined?
11. Can **disagreement between robots** be a signal of hidden structure rather than an error to be averaged away?
12. Can the robot's **own body** be its most informative sensor?
13. Can the fleet hold a **memory no single robot possesses**, built without centralising raw data?

**From the economics/security cluster:**
14. What does a robot's physical presence reveal that **no database records**? (The database stores *intended* state; the robot measures *actual* state. The gap is a measurable, priceable quantity nobody captures.)
15. Can **physical occupancy be made a priced good** — what is the true marginal value of a cubic metre of space-time?
16. Can the robot's **trajectory be a tamper-resistant witness** — physics-based proof of location rather than a trust claim?
17. Can **truth about the physical world be sold as a service**?
18. Can **physical space itself be a cryptographic primitive**?
19. Can the robot's observation make **honesty incentive-compatible** for humans — a mechanism-design instrument rather than surveillance?

**The convergence:** every cluster independently arrived at the same reframe — *the robot is not a thing that moves through a warehouse; it is a thing that can **intervene** in a physical system and thereby learn what observation cannot reveal.* That is the through-line of the five finalists.

## Part 5 — Rounds 5 & 6: patent triage and hostile attack

**Note on Round 5 (patent-oriented analysis).** The brief specified this as a separate round producing, for each finalist: *potential inventive core · potential system combination · potential claim boundary · prior-art risk*. Nothing is omitted; it is presented once, in context.

**Round 6 (hostile attack)** removed two candidates, and the reasons are the most useful part of the exercise.

### ✗ Locomotion as a Seismic Source — REMOVED
**Has this already been invented? Yes.** *Vibroseis* — the vibrating-truck seismic source — has been standard geophysics since the 1950s. A vehicle whose wheels or actuators generate a controlled seismic waveform is precisely this mechanism, at a different scale. The warehouse application is a scale change, not a new mechanism. **Cut.**

### ✗ Homotopic Credential — REMOVED
**Is the mechanism actually novel, or is the topology decoration?** Location-based cryptography already exists. The homotopy-class framing adds robustness to exact routing — but a hostile examiner asks the right question: *does traversing a path class rather than a point sequence produce a different technical effect, or a more convenient one?* On the evidence, it is a usability refinement over point-visits. The underlying primitive (environmental PUF) is already carried by Attestation Channel Capacity. **Cut.**

# #1 — PERMANENT PHYSICAL RED-TEAM

**Score: 87/100**

**One sentence:** A robot that continuously and safely *manufactures* rare physical events in a live warehouse — a toppled pallet, a spill, a darted human — in order to capture the real sensor data that perception and safety systems almost never see.

**The surprising insight:** The scarcest resource in robotics is not compute or data — it is **the tail of the distribution**. Every perception and safety model is starved of rare events, and you cannot wait for them or safely stage them with a fixed rig. But a mobile robot can *physically produce* a controlled version of the rare event, in the real warehouse, under real lighting, and record genuine multimodal ground truth. **The robot manufactures experience.** It closes the sim-to-real gap by making the distribution tail on demand.

**How the robot physically works:** Within a safety envelope, it stages rare-event scenarios using safe props and controlled motions — nudges a tote off-centre, tips a lightweight pallet in a cleared zone, releases a small liquid volume, traverses an unexpected path at an unexpected moment. It records synchronised camera, ultrasonic, proprioceptive and positional ground truth. It then feeds the dataset back to the perception/safety models and *iterates*: the next scenario is chosen to be the one the current model handles worst — making it a genuine adversarial loop, not a fixed test suite.

**Why mobility is essential:** A simulation generates data from an imperfect model of reality — which is exactly where the unknowns are. A fixed test rig cannot stage events across a real, varied warehouse. Only a mobile agent can physically *create* the event in situ and capture the real sensor statistics that follow from it.

**What existing systems do:** Simulation and synthetic data (with a domain gap), or rare-event collection by waiting — which takes years and still under-samples.

**What is fundamentally different:** Existing systems *model* rare events. This one *manufactures* them, physically, with ground truth.

**New information/capability created:** Real-sensor data of rare events **with** ground truth — a scarce, high-value dataset that closes the sim-to-real gap for safety and perception, plus an adversarial loop that keeps finding the events the model still fails.

**Potential patent core:** A method of autonomous, safety-bounded physical generation of rare-event scenarios by a mobile robot, with synchronised multimodal ground-truth capture and adversarial scenario selection driven by the target model's failure surface.

**Potential startup:** Rare-event data-as-a-service for robotics safety; evidence generation for safety cases and regulators.

**Who pays:** Robotics manufacturers (safer perception), safety teams, insurers, regulators (evidence-based safety cases), AV/AMR certification bodies.

**Why people would say "WOW":** *"The robot manufactures the accidents everyone else only simulates — it stages rare events safely, in real life, and records the real data."*

**Biggest technical objection:** *Acceptance, not physics.* Staging rare events in a live operating warehouse is a hard sell to operations even with a safety envelope — and a staged "rare event" is by definition not fully representative of the real one. The mitigation is to stage in cleared zones during low-traffic windows, but the objection is real.

**Prior-art risk: MEDIUM.** Adversarial testing and rare-event simulation are mature in AV/robotics, and physical crash-test facilities exist. The novelty is the *mobile, in-situ, autonomously-iterated* physical generation with ground truth — not adversarial testing in general. This needs a claim-level search.

**Why this deserves further investigation:** It attacks the single most-cited barrier in embodied AI — the scarcity of rare-event data — with a mechanism only a mobile robot can execute, and its output (evidence-grade real rare-event data) is a commodity with a clear buyer.

# #2 — ATTESTATION CHANNEL CAPACITY

**Score: 84/100**

**One sentence:** The robot builds a cryptographically verifiable proof that it was physically at a specific place at a specific time, by hash-chaining the environment's unclonable physical signature into its movement record.

**The surprising insight:** The AMR industry's deepest structural weakness is that **nobody can prove where a robot actually was** — location logs are assertions. But a location is not a coordinate; it is a *physical fingerprint* (RF multipath, ambient magnetic field, acoustic reverberation, thermal gradient) that cannot be recorded elsewhere and replayed. If the robot continuously hashes live environmental signatures into a rolling chain, "where was the robot" stops being a trust claim and becomes a **physics-verified proof**. Escalated: the proof's strength becomes a *quantifiable channel* — how many bits of "where/when" can be attested per metre of travel.

**How the robot physically works:** While driving, it continuously samples multimodal environmental signatures (Wi-Fi/BLE multipath, magnetometer, microphone spectrum, temperature) plus its own micro-motion, and hash-chains signature + timestamp + odometry, anchoring periodically to fixed physical checkpoints. Any past path segment becomes provable after the fact, and the attestation rate is quantified as a channel capacity.

**Why mobility is essential:** A fixed sensor only ever sees one place — it can never produce a *trajectory* proof. The proof is intrinsically a product of movement through a spatially varying field. **To spoof it you must physically reproduce the exact environment at the exact pose — i.e. actually be there.**

**What existing systems do:** GPS (fails indoors), UWB anchors (spoofable and replayable), pure odometry (drifts), and blind trust in the robot's own logs.

**What is fundamentally different:** Location becomes a *physics-backed* fact rather than a recorded assertion — and the strength of that fact is measurable.

**New information/capability created:** A tamper-resistant record of physical presence: provable *who/what was where, when* — for the whole fleet, with quantified assurance.

**Potential patent core:** A method for generating a verifiable trajectory attestation by hash-chaining ambient environmental PUF signatures to odometry, with an associated measure of attestation strength per unit path.

**Potential startup:** Physics-backed chain-of-custody; verified patrol evidence for insurers; auditable provenance for high-value logistics and pharma.

**Who pays:** Insurers (was the patrol real?), regulators, chain-of-custody operators, high-value logistics, auditors, robotics OEMs.

**Why people would say "WOW":** *"The robot can prove it was there — because the room itself signed the receipt."*

**Biggest technical objection:** The **stability-vs-uniqueness tension**. The fingerprint must be stable enough to verify later yet variable enough to be unclonable. Indoor RF multipath is highly dynamic; magnetometer readings drift with nearby metal. If the signature is unstable, verification fails; if it is stable, it may be cloneable.

**Prior-art risk: MEDIUM.** Environmental PUFs and hash-chained audit logs are both established (Palantir's verifiable audit log, Ricoh's tamper-proof camera logs, IBM's secure vehicle data logging). The novelty is the *trajectory-attestation* combination and its quantified capacity. Needs a claim-level search.

**Why this deserves further investigation:** It is a **primitive** — it unlocks a whole family (verified patrol, physical audit, custody witnessing, parametric insurance) and it attacks a weakness every AMR vendor shares, with no incumbent incentive to fix it because their own telemetry is closed.

# #3 — WRITABLE ENVIRONMENT

**Score: 81/100**

**One sentence:** A robot that both powers and *writes* durable state into battery-free backscatter tags embedded in the building — turning the warehouse itself into the robot's external, persistent memory.

**The surprising insight:** Passive tags need no battery — but they need to be **lit**. Fixed readers leave dark zones and give ambiguous geometry. A *moving* illuminator supplies both power and changing angle-of-arrival diversity. Escalate that: if the robot can also **write** to those tags, the environment stops being something the robot merely senses and becomes something it can **program** — a persistent, physical, battery-free memory the robot leaves behind for its future self or its fleet.

**How the robot physically works:** It carries an RF illuminator/reader/writer. As it drives, embedded backscatter tags (strain, moisture, temperature, tamper) modulate the reflected signal; the robot reads ID + sensor value + coarse location, using its changing position to disambiguate. It also *writes*: marking a location's state, recording a passage, setting a flag for a later robot to discover.

**Why mobility is essential:** Static readers cannot reach shadowed or metal-occluded tags and provide no spatial diversity. Only motion delivers illumination *and* geometry where needed — and only a moving agent can write state at the place where the state is relevant.

**What existing systems do:** Fixed RFID portals at doors; battery-powered IoT sensors with a maintenance burden; no write-back to the physical environment.

**What is fundamentally different:** The robot does not just read the environment — it **writes to it**. The building becomes programmable state with no batteries.

**New information/capability created:** Continuous battery-free sensing across the facility **plus** durable physical state the robot can set and later retrieve — environmental memory.

**Potential patent core:** A method of mobile bidirectional backscatter interrogation in which a moving robot illuminates passive tags to both read sensor state and write persistent location-bound state, using positional diversity to resolve geometry.

**Potential startup:** Zero-maintenance facility sensing; physical-digital handover between robots and shifts.

**Who pays:** Facility operators (no battery replacement), insurers (tamper/condition evidence), cold chain (temperature), maintenance teams, multi-robot fleet operators.

**Why people would say "WOW":** *"The robot is a walking power supply and antenna for hundreds of battery-free sensors — and it can write notes into the walls."*

**Biggest technical objection:** **Infrastructure dependence.** Every tag must be pre-installed, must survive the environment, and must be positioned so a driving robot can reach it. Without a tag rollout, the capability is zero — which makes adoption an infrastructure project, not a product purchase.

**Prior-art risk: MEDIUM.** Passive RFID, backscatter sensing and mobile readers are all established. The novel combination is *bidirectional write-back by a mobile robot with positional diversity for a facility-wide battery-free sensor skin*. Needs a claim-level search.

**Why this deserves further investigation:** It converts the robot from a sensor platform into a **programmable interface to the physical world**, with a clear cost story (no battery maintenance) and a natural fleet handover use-case.

# #4 — CONTINUOUS CAUSAL ECONOMY

**Score: 78/100**

**One sentence:** A robot that runs a permanent, economically-priced programme of physical experiments on the warehouse — intervening in real operations and measuring the response — to build a causal model of how the facility actually works.

**The surprising insight:** Every warehouse map ever made is **observational** — it records where things are. Correlation is not causation: nobody knows whether congestion at dock 3 *causes* the backup at aisle 7, or whether both are driven by a hidden third factor like a shift schedule. **The robot is the only thing in the warehouse that can physically execute an intervention** — and therefore the only thing that can discover the warehouse's true causal wiring rather than guess at it. Escalated with mechanism design: each intervention is *priced*, so the robot runs the experiment with the highest economic value of information, continuously.

**How the robot physically works:** In idle windows it runs a library of reversible micro-interventions — pause 8s at a junction, place a tote slightly into an aisle, re-sequence its own pick order, request a door cycle. It reads downstream signals (its own sensors, other robots' telemetry, queue lengths) and fits a causal DAG by intervention-response and difference-in-differences analysis. Each candidate experiment is scored by the expected economic value of the information it would yield.

**Why mobility is essential:** A normal AMR *avoids* disturbance; fixed cameras and the WMS can only observe. Only a mobile agent can introduce a controlled, reversible perturbation at a chosen point and measure the system's response. **Observation alone cannot break confounding.**

**What existing systems do:** Static layout rules, WMS heuristics, engineers' intuition; bottlenecks found by months of trial and error, or by simulation with guessed parameters.

**What is fundamentally different:** It replaces guessed simulation coefficients with *measured causal ones* — and it prices which experiment to run next.

**New information/capability created:** A validated causal graph of warehouse flow — which upstream factors actually drive downstream congestion, and the causal strength of each edge. That information **cannot exist without intervention.**

**Potential patent core:** A method of continuous, safety-bounded, economically-scheduled physical intervention by a mobile robot for causal discovery over warehouse operations, with a value-of-information criterion for experiment selection.

**Potential startup:** Causal operations intelligence; calibrated digital twins whose coefficients are measured rather than guessed.

**Who pays:** Warehouse operators (bottleneck removal), simulation and digital-twin teams (calibration), facility managers (layout investment), industrial engineers.

**Why people would say "WOW":** *"The robot doesn't just drive around the warehouse — it runs experiments on it, and hands you the warehouse's real causal wiring diagram."*

**Biggest technical objection:** **Non-stationarity.** Causal inference assumes a stationary system; warehouses are emphatically not — shift changes, demand swings and ad-hoc human behaviour move the underlying relationships. A causal graph fitted this week may be wrong next week. Mitigated by continuous re-estimation, but never fully solved.

**Prior-art risk: MEDIUM.** Causal discovery, active learning and Bayesian experimental design are all mature, and mobile sensing robots exist. The novel combination is *a mobile robot performing physical interventions in a live warehouse for causal discovery, selected by value of information*. Needs a claim-level search.

**Why this deserves further investigation:** It is the purest expression of "the robot as scientist", it produces knowledge that **cannot be obtained any other way**, and the causal model is a durable asset that appreciates as the experiment programme runs.

# #5 — DISTRIBUTED COHERENT APERTURE

**Score: 76/100**

**One sentence:** Several robots cooperatively synthesise one coherent radar aperture, so the fleet images the warehouse at a resolution no single robot — and no affordable fixed sensor — can achieve.

**The surprising insight:** A 5 cm radar antenna has terrible angular resolution. But a 5 cm antenna **carried 40 m down an aisle becomes a 40 m aperture** — resolution is bought with *motion*, not hardware. Escalate that to the fleet: if several robots maintain **phase coherence** with each other, they form one aperture larger than any of them, and resolution scales with **fleet size rather than hardware cost**. The warehouse does not need a better radar — it needs robots that move.

**How the robot physically works:** Each robot carries one small FMCW radar and records coherent phase against its odometric position while performing ordinary missions. A processor coherently integrates returns across all robots' trajectories to form a 3D reflectivity image. Aisles are traversed from both ends for full coverage.

**Why mobility is essential:** A stationary robot has no aperture — one antenna equals one angular bucket. Only continuous, known-position motion creates the synthetic aperture and the coherent integration gain. And only a *fleet* of mobile platforms can hold a large simultaneous aperture.

**What existing systems do:** Fixed barcode/RFID gates, 2D LiDAR for navigation, occasional handheld cameras. No through-pallet or sub-centimetre imaging.

**What is fundamentally different:** The sensing aperture is *assembled from motion* — and scaled by fleet size — rather than bought as hardware.

**New information/capability created:** Sub-centimetre 3D reflectivity of racks and goods; concealed or mislabelled items; moisture and density anomalies inside pallets. A "radar photograph" of the warehouse that did not previously exist.

**Potential patent core:** A method for cooperative synthetic-aperture imaging in which multiple mobile robots maintain inter-platform phase coherence to form a combined aperture exceeding any single platform's trajectory, with an information-theoretic resolution bound.

**Potential startup:** Through-pallet inventory verification; concealed-item and tamper detection; stock verification for insurers and lenders.

**Who pays:** Warehouse operators (inventory accuracy, concealed-item detection), insurers (stock verification), logistics companies (tamper/loss detection), inventory-backed lenders.

**Why people would say "WOW":** *"The robots don't carry radars — together they ARE one 40-metre radar antenna that happens to walk."*

**Biggest technical assumption — and it is severe:** **Inter-robot phase coherence.** Coherent aperture synthesis requires position knowledge to a fraction of a wavelength — roughly millimetres at warehouse radar frequencies — *shared across platforms*, continuously. Indoor multipath further corrupts phase. This is the hardest engineering problem in the set, and it is the difference between a demonstration and a product.

**Prior-art risk: HIGH.** Synthetic-aperture radar is textbook, and *distributed* / multistatic SAR is an active research field. The novelty is narrow: the warehouse-AMR application and the fleet-coherence method. An examiner would look hard at distributed SAR literature.

**Why this deserves further investigation:** Despite the highest prior-art risk in the set, it produces a genuinely new artefact — an image *through* pallets — and the enabling capability it requires (millimetre inter-robot phase synchronisation) **may itself be the stronger invention**.

## Part 7 — The honest summary

**The five share one idea:** *a mobile robot is not a thing that moves through a space — it is a thing that can intervene in a physical system, and that intervention is what makes new knowledge possible.* Manufactured rare events, provable trajectories, written environments, priced experiments, and coherent fleets are five faces of the same realisation.

**Three things a reviewer should hold against this document:**

1. **Prior-art risk is the weakest link, not the ideas.** Four of the five are built on mature mechanisms (SAR, backscatter, PUFs, causal discovery) applied in a new context. Novel *application* is a harder patent position than novel *mechanism*. Every one needs a claim-level freedom-to-operate search.
2. **The biggest objections are non-technical in three cases.** Acceptance (staging rare events in a live warehouse), infrastructure (the tag rollout for Writable Environment), and non-stationarity (causal inference in a shifting operation) are the real risks — not physics.
3. **The strongest sub-invention may not be the headline concept.** Distributed Coherent Aperture's enabling requirement — millimetre inter-robot phase coherence — is arguably more valuable and more defensible than the imaging application it was invented for.
